## Start with the buyer, not the platform

Every good LinkedIn campaign starts with a clear picture of who you want to reach. Before building anything, I work with clients to define:

- **Target countries and regions,** and whether time zones matter for sales calls.
- **Industries** where you have experience and proof.
- **Company size,** because a ten person startup and a 5,000 person enterprise buy very differently.
- **Job functions and seniority** of the people who decide and the people who influence.
- **What a qualified lead looks like,** agreed with whoever handles sales.

That last point matters more than any targeting setting. If marketing celebrates every form fill and sales finds half of them useless, the campaign will look successful while the business gains nothing.

## Offers that build trust in a Pakistan based vendor

Cold audiences rarely book a sales call from a first ad, especially with a vendor in another country. The offer has to earn the first step. These work well for Islamabad companies:

| Offer | Why it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Free technical audit or code review | Shows expertise before any commitment | Software houses, DevOps and security firms |
| Case study with measurable outcomes | Proves you have done it before | Any B2B service |
| Cost comparison guide for offshore teams | Answers the buyer's biggest question openly | Staffing and outsourcing |
| Short webinar or workshop | Lets buyers meet your experts | Consultancies, training firms |
| Founder or CTO posts as Thought Leader Ads | Puts a real face on the company | Any company with a willing leader |

Being open about time zones, communication, working hours overlap and pricing models also helps. Many buyers have unspoken worries about offshore work. Content that addresses them directly removes friction.

## Ad formats I use most

**Single image and document ads** are reliable for lead generation, especially when the document is a genuinely useful guide or case study.

**Video ads** help buyers see your team and office, which builds trust in a way text cannot.

**Thought Leader Ads** promote posts from real people, such as your founder or technical lead. For Islamabad companies selling abroad, a thoughtful post from the CEO often outperforms a polished company ad.

**Conversation Ads** can work for event invitations and high value offers, but they need careful writing to avoid feeling pushy.

## Lead Gen Forms and follow up across time zones

LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms are pre filled with profile details, which makes signing up easy. That convenience can also bring lower quality leads, so I add one or two qualifying questions, such as company size or project timeline.

The bigger issue for Islamabad companies is speed. A lead in New York that arrives at 3am Pakistan time and gets a reply eight hours later has often moved on. I help clients set up instant email acknowledgements, CRM syncing and a simple routine so the sales team responds as early as possible in the buyer's working day.

## Targeting the Gulf from Islamabad

The UAE and Saudi Arabia are natural markets for many Islamabad firms. They are close, many decision makers already know Pakistani professionals, and time zones overlap well with Pakistani working hours. LinkedIn is widely used by managers and executives in both countries.

A few details make Gulf campaigns work better:

- **Working weeks differ.** The UAE moved to a Saturday and Sunday weekend for government and many private businesses, while Saudi Arabia's weekend is Friday and Saturday. Schedule key campaigns and follow ups around the working days of each market.
- **Ramadan changes everything.** Working hours shorten and buying slows for some services, while others see more activity. Plan budgets and messaging for the month rather than running the same campaign straight through.
- **Credibility signals matter.** Mention relevant regional experience, local partners, time zone overlap and any presence or visits in the Gulf.
- **English works well** for most B2B audiences, though Arabic versions can help for government related or very local audiences.

For many Islamabad software houses and consultancies, the Gulf is the easiest first international market to test on LinkedIn before moving on to the UK or US.

## Budgets and what to expect

LinkedIn clicks usually cost much more than on Facebook or Google. That is fine when the deal value is high. A software project or a long term staffing contract can be worth many times the cost of the campaign that won it. It is not fine when targeting is loose or the offer is weak.

I set expectations around cost per qualified lead and pipeline value, not cost per click. Results are judged over a full sales cycle, which in B2B can take weeks or months.

## Mistakes I see from Islamabad B2B advertisers

1. **Targeting too broadly,** such as "IT" in "United States", which reaches millions of irrelevant people.
2. **Leaving audience expansion and the Audience Network on** without testing.
3. **Asking for a sales call in the first ad** to people who have never heard of you.
4. **No company page credibility,** with few posts, no case studies and an outdated profile.
5. **Slow follow up** because leads arrive outside Pakistani working hours.
6. **Judging success by likes and clicks** rather than meetings and pipeline.

## Is your company page ready for paid traffic?

Before spending on LinkedIn ads, look at your company page and your leaders' profiles the way a buyer in London or Dubai would. Many will check them before replying to anything.

- **A clear headline** that says what you do and for whom, not just "Software Company".
- **Recent posts,** ideally weekly, with real work, team news and useful insights.
- **Case studies or client results** you have permission to share.
- **Founder and senior team profiles** that are complete, professional and active.
- **Employees linked to the page,** which shows a real team.
- **A website that matches,** with clear services, contact details and proof of work.

If the page looks empty or outdated, fix it first. Ads that send buyers to a weak page waste money, however good the targeting.

## A 90 day LinkedIn plan for an Islamabad software house

Here is a practical structure for a software house in Islamabad targeting mid sized companies in the UK. It is an example, but the shape works for most B2B firms.

**Month 1: build awareness and an audience.** Run Thought Leader Ads from the founder and short video ads introducing the team, targeted at technology and operations leaders in the chosen industries. Build retargeting audiences from video viewers and page engagers.

**Month 2: offer value.** Retarget engaged people with a useful document ad, such as a guide to costs and timelines for outsourcing a project, collected with a short Lead Gen Form. Follow up quickly and personally.

**Month 3: invite a conversation.** Show case studies and a clear offer, such as a free technical consultation, to people who downloaded the guide or engaged repeatedly. Sales reaches out directly to the most promising accounts.

Across all three months, track which audiences produce meetings and deals, not just leads, and move budget toward them.
