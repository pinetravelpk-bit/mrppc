## Why you need someone who understands the target market

One of the biggest mistakes Pakistan based sellers make is researching keywords the way they would search themselves. A shopper in Texas or Manchester uses different words, spellings and phrases. "Shalwar" means nothing to most American shoppers. "Sports bra" and "workout top" are not the same search in the US.

A good Amazon Ads expert researches keywords from the shopper's point of view in the target marketplace. That means using Amazon's own search suggestions, search term reports, competitor listings and third party research tools, then grouping keywords by intent and relevance before building campaigns.

## Sponsored ad types and when to use them

**Sponsored Products** appear in search results and on product pages. They are the foundation of nearly every account and usually take most of the budget.

**Sponsored Brands** show your logo, a custom headline and several products, or a video, at the top of search results. They need Brand Registry and are excellent for brand searches and category keywords.

**Sponsored Display** reaches shoppers on product pages and, in some cases, beyond Amazon. I mainly use it to retarget people who viewed your products and to defend your own listings from competitors.

## Brand Registry and why it matters

Brand Registry unlocks Sponsored Brands, most Sponsored Display options, A+ Content and better brand protection. To enrol, you need a registered or pending trademark from an office Amazon accepts. Many Pakistan based sellers file a trademark in the target marketplace, such as the US or UK, for this reason.

It takes time and some cost, but for any serious private label seller, I recommend it. Without it, you are competing with one hand tied behind your back.

## Working across time zones from Islamabad

Pakistan Standard Time is several hours ahead of the US and a few hours ahead of the UK. That affects how campaigns are managed.

Shoppers in the US are most active during their daytime, which is evening and night in Islamabad. Budget caps that run out in the US morning mean your ads disappear when shoppers are most active. I set budgets and any bid scheduling around the marketplace's local time, not ours.

## Which products suit Amazon advertising

Not every product is a good candidate for paid ads on Amazon. Before launching, I look at a few questions with every seller.

**Is there enough margin?** After the selling price, Amazon fees, product cost and shipping, there must be room left for advertising. Products with very thin margins struggle to ever become profitable with ads.

**Is there real search demand?** If shoppers are not searching for the product, Sponsored Products have little to work with. Amazon's own search bar suggestions and search term data are a good first check.

**Can you compete on the first page?** Look at the listings you would appear next to. If every competitor has thousands of reviews and a lower price, you need a clear reason to be chosen, such as better quality, a unique feature or a stronger brand story.

**Is the product easy to ship and stock?** Heavy, fragile or seasonal products add risk. Long shipping times from Pakistan make stock planning especially important.

Products that pass these checks give advertising a real chance. Products that fail them usually need changes before ads can help.

## Coupons, deals and ads working together

Advertising rarely works alone on Amazon. Coupons, Lightning Deals, Prime exclusive discounts and seasonal events all change how shoppers respond to your ads.

A coupon badge on a sponsored listing can lift click rate noticeably, because the discount is visible right in the search results. During a launch, a modest coupon combined with Sponsored Products often helps a new product win its first sales faster than ads alone.

Big events such as Prime Day, Black Friday and Cyber Monday need special planning. Competition and costs per click rise sharply, so I raise budgets ahead of time for products with deals running and lower bids on products without them. Afterwards, budgets come back down so the account does not keep paying event prices on normal days.

The key is coordination. Your promotions calendar and your advertising plan should be the same document, not two separate ideas.

## What a good monthly Amazon PPC report shows

A useful report answers business questions, not just advertising ones. I include:

- Ad spend, ad sales and ACoS by product, compared with the break even point.
- Total sales and TACoS, showing whether ads are growing the whole business.
- Top converting search terms and new keywords moved into exact match.
- Wasted search terms that were added as negatives.
- Organic rank movement for key keywords.
- Stock warnings and planned changes for next month.

If your current reports only show clicks and impressions, ask for more. You cannot manage what you cannot see.
