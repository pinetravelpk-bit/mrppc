## What a PPC expert actually does

PPC stands for pay per click. You pay the platform, such as Google or Facebook, when someone clicks your ad or takes another action. A PPC expert is the person who decides where that money goes and makes sure it comes back as customers.

In practice, the job has five parts:

1. **Strategy.** Choosing the right platforms, campaign types, locations and budgets for your goal.
2. **Setup.** Building campaigns, writing ads, creating audiences and connecting conversion tracking.
3. **Measurement.** Making sure every call, form, WhatsApp message and sale is counted correctly.
4. **Optimisation.** Reviewing search terms, placements, audiences and creative every week, cutting waste and testing improvements.
5. **Reporting.** Explaining in plain language what you spent, what you got back and what happens next.

If someone only talks about the second part, setup, be careful. Anyone can launch a campaign. The value of an expert sits in measurement and optimisation, the slow, steady work that turns a campaign from expensive to profitable.

## What does a PPC expert in Islamabad cost?

There are two separate costs, and you should always see them separately.

- **Ad spend** goes directly to Google, Meta or the other platform. You set this budget, and it should be charged to your own card in your own account.
- **Management fees** pay the expert for strategy, setup and ongoing work.

Management pricing in Islamabad varies a lot. Some charge a fixed monthly fee, some charge a percentage of ad spend, and some offer one time setup packages. What matters is that the fee matches the work. A single Google search campaign for a local clinic needs far less time than five platforms for an ecommerce brand selling to the UK.

Be wary of very cheap offers that bundle ad spend and management into one package price. If you cannot see exactly how much went to Google, you cannot judge whether you are getting value. ## Eight questions to ask before you hire anyone

Bring these to your first meeting. A real expert answers them easily. Someone who is mainly selling will struggle.

1. **Will the ad accounts be in my name?** They should be. Your account history, data and audiences belong to your business.
2. **Who exactly will manage my campaigns each week?** Get a name, not "our team".
3. **How will you track calls, forms and WhatsApp leads?** If the answer is only "clicks and impressions", walk away.
4. **What will you report on, and how often?** Ask to see a sample report.
5. **How do you reduce wasted spend?** Listen for search term reviews, negative keywords, placement exclusions and audience controls.
6. **Which platform would you start with for my business, and why?** A good expert asks about your customers before answering.
7. **What happens if we stop working together?** You should keep everything, with no lock in.
8. **What do you need from me?** Good results need your input on offers, customer questions and lead quality.

## Red flags I see again and again

Some warning signs come up so often in Islamabad that they are worth listing on their own.

- **Guaranteed first position on Google.** Nobody controls the auction. Position one is not even always profitable.
- **Guaranteed number of leads before they have seen your account.** Results depend on your offer, market, website and budget.
- **Reports full of reach and impressions with no mention of enquiries or sales.**
- **Ad accounts created in the manager's own business name.** If you part ways, you lose everything.
- **No questions about your margins or customer value.** Without those numbers, nobody can tell you whether a campaign is profitable.
- **Boosting posts presented as a full Facebook strategy.** Boosting has its uses, but it is not campaign management.
