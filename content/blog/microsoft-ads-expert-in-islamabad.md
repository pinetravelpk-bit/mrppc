That means office workers, managers, procurement teams and business owners researching suppliers during the day. For B2B companies, that is exactly who you want to reach.

## Scheduling around overseas office hours

This is where Islamabad based advertisers often go wrong. Your ads should run when your buyers are awake and working, not when your team is.

Pakistan is several hours ahead of the UK and many hours ahead of the US. A software house in I-9 that runs ads during Pakistani office hours is advertising to American buyers in the middle of the night.

## Writing search ads for overseas B2B buyers

Search ads have very little space, so every word has to work. For Islamabad companies selling abroad, the best performing ads usually answer the buyer's practical worries before they click.

- **Lead with the service and the outcome,** such as "Custom Software Development, Delivered On Time", not your company name.
- **Address time zones directly.** Lines like "UK hours overlap" or "US time zone support" reassure buyers who fear slow communication.
- **Show proof briefly,** such as years in business, number of projects or a recognised certification, if you can back it up.
- **Give a sense of value** with phrases like "Transparent pricing" or "Fixed price projects available".
- **Make the next step easy,** for example "Book a free 30 minute consultation".

Use the ad assets Microsoft offers too: sitelinks to case studies and pricing pages, callouts for key strengths, and structured snippets listing your services. Every extra line of relevant information makes your ad larger and more useful than a competitor's.

Avoid vague claims like "world class" or "best in Pakistan" in ads. Buyers abroad tend to trust specific, checkable statements far more than superlatives.

## Landing pages that convince overseas buyers

A buyer in Chicago or Leeds clicking an ad from an Islamabad company often has one unspoken question: can I trust a team I have never met, in a country I have never visited? Your landing page has to answer it before they leave.

The pages that convert best for Pakistani exporters usually include:

- **Clear service pages** for each offer, not one general "services" page.
- **Proof first.** Case studies, client logos you have permission to use, reviews from platforms like Clutch or Google, and named testimonials.
- **Working hours and communication.** Show which hours overlap with the buyer's time zone and how you communicate, such as daily standups, Slack or weekly reports.
- **Pricing guidance.** Even a range or a "projects typically start from" line removes a big barrier.
- **Team faces.** Photos and short bios of the people the client will actually work with.
- **A simple next step,** such as booking a call through a calendar that shows times in the visitor's own time zone.
- **Fast loading for overseas visitors,** with hosting or a content delivery network that serves pages quickly in your target markets.

When these are in place, every pound or dollar spent on Microsoft Ads works harder.

## A 60 day test plan

For an Islamabad company trying Microsoft Ads for the first time, I usually suggest this structure.

**Weeks 1 to 2.** Check search demand for your services on Bing in each target country. Set up the UET tag and conversion goals. Import or build a small number of tightly themed search campaigns for your best services.

**Weeks 3 to 4.** Launch with schedules set to buyer hours and a modest budget. Review search terms closely and add negatives, especially job related searches like "developer jobs" or "salary".

**Weeks 5 to 6.** Add LinkedIn profile bid adjustments for your key industries and job functions. Compare cost per lead with Google Ads for the same services.

**Weeks 7 to 8.** Keep what works, cut what does not and decide whether Microsoft deserves a permanent place in your budget.

Eight weeks is usually enough to see whether the channel can deliver qualified enquiries at a sensible cost for your business.

A final practical point: keep Microsoft and Google reporting side by side in the same monthly report, using the same definitions of a lead. When both channels are judged on qualified enquiries and cost per qualified enquiry, it becomes easy to see where the next rupee or dollar should go, and to shift budget between them as results change month to month.

## Mistakes I see from Islamabad advertisers

1. **Targeting Pakistan on Bing** for local services, where Google is the better choice.
2. **Running ads in Pakistani office hours** for buyers in the US or UK.
3. **Daily automatic imports** from Google that copy every problem.
4. **No UET tag,** so automated bidding has nothing to learn from.
5. **Ignoring LinkedIn profile targeting** for B2B campaigns.
6. **Sending overseas buyers to a slow website** hosted far from their market, or one that does not explain pricing, time zones and communication clearly.
