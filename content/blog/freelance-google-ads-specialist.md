## Why businesses choose a freelancer

The main advantages are simple.

- **Direct access.** You speak to the person who actually manages your account, not an account manager passing messages along.
- **Senior skill at a fair cost.** You pay for expertise, not office overheads and layers of management.
- **Speed.** One decision maker means changes happen quickly.
- **Flexibility.** Scope can grow or shrink with your business.

The trade off is capacity. A freelancer has limited hours, so the good ones are selective about how many clients they take on. That is a good sign, not a bad one.

## How freelance Google Ads specialists charge

Freelancers use a few common pricing models.

- **Monthly management fee.** A fixed amount for ongoing management of agreed campaigns. The most common model for ongoing work.
- **Hourly rate.** Flexible, often used on marketplaces. Ask for an estimate of monthly hours so costs stay predictable.
- **Setup or project fee.** A one-time price for building an account, fixing tracking or running an audit.
- **Percentage of ad spend.** Sometimes used for larger budgets. Make sure budget decisions stay with you.

Very cheap offers usually mean very little time is spent on your account. Google Ads improves through regular reviews, and those take time. Ask exactly what is included each month.

Remember that ad spend is separate. It should be charged by Google directly to your card, never routed through the freelancer.

## Account ownership: the most important rule

This is where most freelance hiring disasters begin. The Google Ads account must belong to your business.

In practice, that means:

1. **You create the account** in your business name, or keep your existing one.
2. **Your card pays Google directly.** You receive the invoices.
3. **You invite the freelancer** as a user through the account's access settings, or link their manager account, with the access level you choose.
4. **You never share your Google login password.** Access through user invites is safer and can be removed instantly.
5. **You keep everything if you part ways:** campaign history, audiences, conversion data and reports.

The same applies to Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager and Google Business Profile. Own them, then grant access.

## What to agree in writing

You do not need a long legal contract, but a short written agreement prevents most problems. Include:

- **Scope.** Which campaigns, platforms and tasks are included each month.
- **Reporting.** How often you get reports and what they cover.
- **Communication.** How and when you can reach the freelancer, especially across time zones.
- **Fees and payment terms,** including how changes in scope are priced.
- **Notice period** for either side to end the arrangement.
- **Confidentiality** of your data and business information.
- **Ownership** of all accounts, assets and ad copy created for you.

## How to check a freelancer before hiring

1. **Ask for a live account review.** A real specialist can look at your account, or a demo account, and explain what they see and what they would change.
2. **Ask about their first 30 days.** Tracking and wasted spend should be near the top of the list.
3. **Ask how they report.** Request a sample report with sensitive details removed.
4. **Ask about time zones.** For overseas clients working with a Pakistan based freelancer, agree regular overlap hours.
5. **Check references or reviews,** ideally from businesses similar to yours.
6. **Start with a defined first step,** such as an audit or a one month trial, before a long commitment.

## Your first week with a new freelancer

A smooth start saves weeks later. Here is a simple onboarding checklist I use with new clients, and one you can ask any freelancer to follow.

- **Access granted** to Google Ads, Google Analytics, Tag Manager and, for local businesses, Google Business Profile, all through user invites.
- **A kickoff call** covering your products or services, margins, best customers, target areas and what a good lead looks like.
- **Current results recorded** before any changes, so improvement can be measured honestly later.
- **Tracking checked** for calls, forms, WhatsApp clicks and sales, with any gaps listed.
- **A written 30 day plan** with priorities in order.
- **Reporting and communication agreed,** including which day reports arrive and how quickly messages are answered.
- **A lead feedback routine,** so you can tell the freelancer which enquiries were good and which were not.

If a freelancer skips most of these and jumps straight into making changes, ask why.

## Red flags

- They want to create the account in their own name or ask for your password.
- They promise a guaranteed number of leads or a guaranteed top position.
- They cannot explain how they will track calls, forms or sales.
- Their reports only show impressions and clicks.
- They want your ad budget paid to them rather than to Google.
- They go quiet for weeks with no visible changes in the account history.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to hire a freelance Google Ads specialist?

Yes, if you keep the account in your own name, billed to your card, and give the freelancer user access instead of your password. Agree scope and reporting in writing.

### How much does a freelance Google Ads specialist charge?

Freelancers charge monthly fees, hourly rates, setup fees or occasionally a percentage of spend. Ad spend is always separate and paid directly to Google.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an agency for Google Ads?

A freelancer gives direct access to the expert and lower overheads. An agency offers a larger team. If your main need is senior Google Ads skill and quick decisions, a freelancer is often better value.

### Can a freelancer in Pakistan manage Google Ads for UK or US businesses?

Yes. Google Ads targeting works by the customer's location, not the manager's. Agree overlap hours, communication channels and reporting in advance.

### What access should I give a freelance Google Ads specialist?

Invite them as a user with standard or admin access, depending on the work, or link their manager account. Never share your Google login password.
