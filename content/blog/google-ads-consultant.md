## Consultant, manager or agency: what is the difference?

The three roles overlap, but they solve different problems.

- **A Google Ads manager or specialist** runs your account day to day. They make the changes, review data and report back.
- **An agency** provides a team, often including design and content, and usually runs the account on your behalf.
- **A Google Ads consultant** advises. They diagnose problems, set strategy, review other people's work and teach, while your own team or another provider handles the daily changes.

Many consultants, including me, also manage accounts. The point is that consulting is a different service. You are paying for judgment and direction rather than hours of execution.

## What a Google Ads consultant audit covers

An audit is often the first step. A good one goes far beyond running an automated checker. Here is what I look at.

**Conversion tracking.** Are the right actions counted? Are calls and WhatsApp tracked? Are any conversions double counted? This is usually where the biggest problems hide.

**Account structure.** Are campaigns organised by service, product or location in a way that allows control? Are brand and non brand searches separated?

**Keywords and negatives.** What searches are actually triggering ads? How much is spent on irrelevant terms?

**Ads and assets.** Do ads match the searches they appear for? Are sitelinks, callouts, call assets and images in place?

**Landing pages.** Does each ad lead to a relevant, fast page with a clear next step?

**Bidding and budgets.** Is the bidding strategy suited to the account's data? Are the best campaigns limited by budget while weaker ones spend freely?

The result should be a clear report written for business owners, with problems ranked by impact and practical steps your team or provider can follow.

## How Google Ads consultants charge

Consultants use a few common pricing models. Each has pros and cons.

- **Fixed price audit.** You know the cost upfront. Good for a first engagement.
- **Hourly or daily rate.** Flexible for strategy sessions and training, but less predictable.
- **Monthly retainer.** A set number of reviews and support each month. Good for ongoing guidance.
- **Percentage of ad spend.** More common for full management than pure consulting. It can create an incentive to push spend up, so ask how decisions about budget are made.

The right model depends on what you need. For most businesses, I suggest starting with a fixed price audit. It shows you how the consultant thinks before you commit to anything ongoing.

## Example: a software house in Islamabad entering the UK market

To show how consulting works in practice, here is a hypothetical engagement. The details are illustrative, but the shape is typical.

A software house in the IT parks has a marketer who runs Google Ads for local leads. The founders now want enterprise clients in the UK, and they are about to spend far more than before. They do not want to hand everything to an agency, but they also do not want to learn by burning budget.

**Week 1: audit and strategy session.** The consultant reviews the existing account, checks tracking, and runs a session with the founders and marketer on target industries, services, deal sizes and what a qualified UK lead looks like.

**Week 2: plan.** The marketer receives a written plan: separate UK campaigns, ad schedules set to UK business hours, landing pages that show case studies and time zone overlap, offline conversion imports from the CRM, and a sensible test budget.

**Weeks 3 to 8: monthly advisory.** The marketer builds and runs the campaigns. The consultant reviews search terms, lead quality and changes every two weeks and answers questions in between.

The company keeps control and builds its own skills, while avoiding the expensive mistakes most teams make in their first international campaigns.

## How to get the most from a consultant

- **Give access early.** Add the consultant to your Google Ads and Analytics accounts with read or standard access before the first meeting.
- **Share business context.** Margins, best customers, sales cycle and what a good lead looks like matter as much as the account data.
- **Involve whoever will make the changes.** If your in house marketer or agency attends the review, fixes happen faster.
- **Ask for priorities, not just problems.** A list of fifty issues is less useful than the five that matter most.
- **Review progress after a month.** A short follow-up keeps the plan on track.
