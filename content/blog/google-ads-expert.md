## What a Google Ads expert actually does

Most people imagine the job is "setting up ads". Setup is maybe ten percent of it. The rest is a steady cycle of reading data and making careful decisions. In a typical account I manage, the work looks like this.

**Every few days:** check spend against budget, look for sudden changes in cost or conversions, and make sure nothing has broken, such as tracking, a landing page or a disapproved ad.

**Every week:** review the search terms report, add negative keywords, check which ads and assets are winning, and adjust bids or targets where the data is clear.

**Every month:** review lead quality with the client, test new ad angles or landing pages, check location and device performance, and write a plain language report on what changed and what comes next.

**Every quarter:** step back and look at strategy. Are we in the right campaign types? Is the budget split sensible? Are there new products, services or areas to add?

None of this is glamorous. All of it adds up.

## The search terms report: where experts earn their fee

If I had to pick one task that separates an expert from a beginner, it would be the search terms report. This report shows the actual words people typed before they clicked your ad. Not your keywords, their searches.

With today's broad matching, those two things can be very different. You might bid on "dentist Islamabad" and find your ads showing for "dentist jobs Islamabad", "free dental checkup" or "dental course Pakistan". Every one of those clicks costs money and almost none of them will ever become a patient.

## What an expert adds now that Google's AI does the bidding

Some people ask whether experts still matter now that Google automates so much. Smart bidding sets bids in real time. Performance Max decides where ads appear. Broad match and newer AI features find searches you never thought of.

The honest answer is that automation changed the job, it did not remove it. The algorithm optimises toward whatever you tell it is a success, using whatever data you give it. If your tracking counts a "Contact us" page view as a conversion, Google will find you more page views. If you never exclude job seekers, Google will happily find more job seekers.

An expert's job today is to:

- **Feed the machine the right goals,** through accurate conversion tracking and sensible conversion values.
- **Put guardrails around it,** with negative keywords, brand exclusions, location settings and budget controls.
- **Give it good raw material,** such as relevant ads, strong images, sitelinks and fast landing pages.
- **Watch what it does,** and step in quickly when it drifts toward cheap but useless traffic.

Automation is a powerful engine. An expert is the driver.

## How to check a Google Ads expert before you hire

Claims are easy. These checks are harder to fake.

1. **Ask for a live walkthrough.** A real expert can share their screen and talk you through how they would review your account: search terms, tracking, structure and settings. Listen for specifics.
2. **Ask what they would do in the first 30 days.** You want a clear order of priorities, usually starting with tracking and wasted spend.
3. **Ask how they handle Google's recommendations.** If the answer is "we apply them all", keep looking.
4. **Ask who owns the account.** It should be your account, billed to your card, with the expert added as a user.
5. **Ask to see a sample report.** It should talk about leads, sales and next actions, not just impressions.
6. **Check certifications, but do not stop there.** Google offers free certifications through Skillshop. They show knowledge, not results, so treat them as a starting point.
