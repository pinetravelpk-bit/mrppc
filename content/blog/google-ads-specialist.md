## Specialist or all-round digital marketer?

A good all-round digital marketer is valuable. They can plan content, manage social media, write emails and keep a website updated. But nobody can be a deep expert in everything.

The difference shows up in the details. A generalist may set up a search campaign with broad keywords and a "maximise conversions" bid strategy, because that is what Google suggests. A specialist will first ask what counts as a conversion, whether tracking is reliable, how much data the account has, which searches are already wasting money and whether the budget is enough for automated bidding to learn.

For small budgets, a generalist can be fine. Once Google Ads becomes a meaningful source of leads or sales, a specialist usually pays for themselves through waste removed and better results.

## Skill 1: keyword match types

Match types decide which searches can trigger your ads. Getting them wrong is one of the most common and expensive mistakes I see.

**Broad match** reaches the widest range of related searches. With good conversion data and smart bidding it can find valuable new searches, but without negatives it can also match things like courses, jobs and DIY queries.

**Phrase match** shows ads when a search includes the meaning of your phrase. It balances reach and control well for many local businesses.

**Exact match** shows ads for searches with the same meaning as your keyword. It gives the most control and is often where proven keywords end up.

A specialist uses all three deliberately, often starting with tighter control and opening up as data and negative keyword lists grow.

## Skill 2: conversion tracking

If Google cannot see which clicks became customers, every automated decision it makes is a guess. A specialist treats tracking as the foundation of the account. That usually means:

- **Google Tag Manager** to manage tags cleanly.
- **Google Analytics 4** linked to Google Ads, with key events marked properly.
- **Separate conversion actions** for form submissions, phone calls, WhatsApp clicks and purchases, each with a sensible value.
- **Call tracking** for calls from ads and from the website, counted only when they last long enough to be real enquiries.
- **Enhanced conversions** to improve accuracy as browsers limit tracking.
- **Offline conversion imports** where sales close later by phone or in person, so Google learns from real customers.
- **Consent Mode** where you advertise to the UK or Europe.

In Pakistan, where a large share of leads arrive by phone and WhatsApp, an account that tracks only forms is blind to most of its results.

## Skill 3: choosing the right bidding strategy

Google offers several bidding strategies. Each suits a different stage of an account.

| Bidding strategy | What Google optimises for | When a specialist uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Manual CPC | Your chosen bid per click | Rarely now, mainly for tight control in tiny or unusual accounts |
| Maximise clicks | As many clicks as possible | New accounts gathering early data, with a bid cap |
| Maximise conversions | As many conversions as possible | Once tracking is reliable and some conversions exist |
| Target CPA | Conversions at a target cost each | When there is steady conversion volume and a known affordable cost per lead |
| Maximise conversion value | Highest total value | Ecommerce or leads with different values |
| Target ROAS | Value at a target return | Ecommerce with enough sales data and clear margins |

The skill is not knowing the list. It is knowing when an account is ready to move from one to the next, and when a change has gone wrong.

## Skill 4: campaign types and structure

A specialist knows what each campaign type is good at. Search captures active demand. Shopping suits online stores. Performance Max can scale across Google's networks when tracking and assets are strong. Demand Gen and YouTube build interest before people search. Display is mostly useful for remarketing.

Structure matters just as much. A clean account separates brand searches from non brand, groups keywords by service or product, and keeps locations and budgets easy to control. When I take over messy accounts, restructuring alone often brings a noticeable improvement.

## What a Google Ads specialist should do in the first 30 days

Here is the plan I follow with a new client. Your specialist's plan may differ in detail, but the order should feel similar.

**Days 1 to 5: audit and listen.** Review the account, tracking, search terms, settings and landing pages. Talk to you about your margins, best customers and what a good lead looks like.

**Days 6 to 10: fix the foundations.** Correct conversion tracking first. Then fix the most expensive problems, such as wrong location settings, missing negatives or broken pages.

**Days 11 to 20: rebuild where needed.** Restructure campaigns and ad groups, rewrite ads with stronger relevance, add assets like sitelinks and call buttons, and set an appropriate bidding strategy.

**Days 21 to 30: watch closely and report.** Review search terms every few days, add negatives, pause weak ads and share a first report covering what changed, early results and the plan for month two.
