## One idea, several cuts

The biggest creative mistake I see is making a single video and running it everywhere. YouTube has several formats, and each one needs a different shape and length.

A typical plan from one shoot might include:

- **A 30 to 60 second horizontal version** for skippable in-stream ads, with the brand and hook in the first five seconds.
- **A 6 second bumper** that repeats the key message for people who have already seen the longer ad.
- **A vertical 15 to 30 second cut** for YouTube Shorts, fast paced with captions.
- **A thumbnail and title** designed for in-feed ads, where people choose to click and watch.

Planning these cuts before filming saves money and gives every format a fair chance.

## The first five seconds

On skippable in-stream ads, viewers can skip after five seconds. In many campaign types you only pay when someone watches long enough or interacts. A strong opening therefore does two things: it keeps the right people watching and it saves you money on the ones who leave.

For Islamabad audiences, openings that work include a local question ("Looking for a flat in Islamabad under 1.5 crore?"), a recognisable place, a familiar face, or a direct problem statement. Language matters too. Urdu or a natural mix of Urdu and English often feels more personal for local and family focused audiences.

## YouTube Shorts for local brands

Shorts have changed how many people in Pakistan use YouTube. Younger viewers in particular now spend a lot of time swiping through vertical videos, much like on TikTok or Instagram Reels. Ads that appear between Shorts reach this audience in a format that feels natural to them.

Shorts ads work best when they follow the same rules as good short form content:

- **Hook in the first second,** with movement, a face or a bold line of text.
- **Vertical framing,** filmed for a phone screen rather than cropped from a horizontal video.
- **Captions on screen,** since many viewers watch without sound.
- **Fast pacing,** with quick cuts and one clear message.
- **A simple call to action** that fits the platform, such as "Visit our F-7 branch this weekend".

For Islamabad businesses that already make Reels or TikToks, Shorts ads are an easy extension. The same idea, edited for YouTube, can reach people who are not on those other platforms or who use YouTube more. ## Measuring what video actually does

Judging YouTube only by direct clicks makes it look weaker than it is. Many people watch an ad and then search your brand on Google days later, or visit your Instagram first.

I track:

- Leads and calls from YouTube traffic, through Google Ads and GA4 conversion tracking.
- View rates and audience retention, to see which openings hold attention.
- Growth in branded searches and direct website visits during campaigns.
- Results from remarketing to video viewers on Search and display.

Video viewers make excellent remarketing audiences. I usually follow up with search and [display remarketing](/services/display-remarketing/) campaigns aimed at people who watched most of a video.

Keep one more thing in mind. YouTube results often show up in other channels first. During a good video campaign, it is common to see more people searching your brand name on Google, more profile visits on Instagram and more direct WhatsApp messages. Track those signals alongside YouTube's own reports, so the campaign gets credit for the demand it creates.

## Example: an admissions campaign for a school in Islamabad

Here is how a YouTube campaign might look for a hypothetical private school in E-11 preparing for admissions season. The details are illustrative.

**Goal.** More admission enquiries from parents within a reasonable commute of the campus, in the six weeks before the admission deadline.

**Creative.** One filming day produces a 45 second campus tour led by the principal, a 6 second bumper with the line "Admissions open, visit our E-11 campus", a vertical Shorts cut of a classroom moment, and a short parent testimonial.

**Targeting.** Parents in Islamabad and nearby Rawalpindi areas, custom segments of people who searched for schools and admissions on Google, and remarketing to visitors of the school website.

**Sequence.** The campus tour runs first as a skippable in-stream ad. People who watch most of it later see the testimonial and the bumper, then search and display ads inviting them to book a visit.

**Measurement.** Campus visit bookings and enquiry calls, tracked through Google Ads and GA4, plus the increase in people searching the school's name during the campaign.

The structure works for many other businesses too, such as property projects, clinics and wedding venues. One good shoot, several cuts, a planned sequence and honest measurement.

## Filming in Islamabad on a sensible budget

You do not need a large production company to make effective YouTube ads. Many of the best performing local ads are filmed with a modern phone or a simple camera, a clip on microphone and good natural light.

A few practical tips:

- **Sound matters more than picture.** People forgive average video. They do not forgive bad audio.
- **Film real places.** Your premises, your team and your city build trust. Islamabad's greenery, the Margalla Hills and familiar markets make a recognisable backdrop.
- **Plan every cut before you film,** including vertical shots for Shorts, so you are not cropping awkwardly later.
- **Keep scripts short and natural.** Speak the way your customers speak, in Urdu, English or both.
- **Film more than you need.** Extra shots make new versions possible when the first ones tire.

## Mistakes I see from Islamabad advertisers on YouTube

1. **Running a TV commercial unchanged** on every format.
2. **Hiding the brand until the end** of a skippable ad.
3. **No captions,** even though many people watch on mute.
4. **Targeting all of Pakistan** for a business that only serves the twin cities.
5. **Letting ads run on unsuitable channels,** such as kids content, without reviewing placements.
6. **Judging YouTube only on direct clicks** and cutting it before measuring its effect on search.
